\subsection{Rank-eligibility witness: the acceptance set is non-empty}\label{sec:witness}

The baseline board of \cref{sec:f1} contains no rank-eligible policy, leaving open whether the conjunction can be satisfied at all. We answer by construction with an out-of-band oracle, not an entrant. For each seed, the script replays the deterministic, policy-invariant tape one bar ahead and mixes standardized next-bar truth $z_t$ with one fixed seeded noise path $\varepsilon_t$:
\[
\mathrm{signal}_t(s)=s z_t+\sqrt{1-s^2}\,\varepsilon_t,
\qquad 0\le s\le1.
\]
The same $\varepsilon_t$ is reused at every $s$ (common random numbers), so changing signal strength does not silently change the experiment. A sign-following policy trades every bar; a deadband policy trades only when $|\mathrm{signal}|>1$ and otherwise holds. Both are scored through the unchanged SharpeBench conjunction over 16 seeds. The script first checks the full coarse acceptance set. It refines a crossing by bisection only when coarse eligibility is monotone; otherwise it reports the observed set without calling any crossing a threshold. All ten attained series are monotone after the common-random-number correction.

The result is an existence proof and a calibration, not evidence about an agent. The deadband construction is eligible on every tier, so the acceptance set is non-empty. The every-seed gate is last to open at all ten observed thresholds; pooled DSR and bootstrap gates open earlier. The sign follower never becomes eligible on Calm, even at $s=1$, because its every-bar turnover costs more than the low-volatility signal is worth. Thus the environment does not impose an activity requirement, but its cost model does require a Calm policy to ration turnover.
