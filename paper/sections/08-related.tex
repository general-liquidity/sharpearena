\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Environment interfaces.} Gym \citep{brockman2016gym} demonstrated that a small, stable environment interface is worth more than any single environment, and Gymnasium \citep{towers2024gymnasium} carried the interface forward with versioned environment IDs and a conformance checker; SharpeArena's Python surface is a first-class Gymnasium citizen with versioned, namespaced IDs and adopts its \texttt{check\_env}. PettingZoo \citep{terry2021pettingzoo} standardized the multi-agent case, which SharpeArena's shared-book and batched-competition environments implement. Minari \citep{minari} standardizes offline-RL datasets; SharpeArena exports leak-safe rollouts to it. The wire contract of \cref{sec:contract} differs from all of these in scope: it is a language-agnostic JSON boundary for external agent processes, governed like a protocol rather than a library API.

\paragraph{Evaluation rigor in RL.} The environment's evaluation stance imports three lessons from the RL evaluation literature. From the ALE revisitation \citep{machado2018revisiting}: determinism in the environment invites trajectory memorization, so evaluation must be designed against it; SharpeArena keeps determinism for verifiability and defeats memorization with procedural scenario distributions and held-out seed bands instead of injected stochasticity, and \cref{sec:predictability} measures how far that gets. From Procgen \citep{cobbe2020procgen}: procedural generation over integer seed intervals makes generalization measurable, which \cref{sec:scenarios} ports to markets and \cref{sec:protocol-bands} states as a protocol. Prioritized Level Replay \citep{jiang2021prioritized} shows seed-level curricula shape what agents learn, which motivates keeping the train band operator-owned and the held-out band frozen. $\tau$-bench \citep{yao2024taubench} argues that agent reliability must be measured across repeated runs rather than on average; that requirement lives in the SharpeBench kernel this environment feeds \citep{toca2026sharpebench}.

The table compresses five substantive differences. FinRL and FinRL-Meta wrap historical market data into gym-style training environments at scale, and FinRL-Meta's DataOps pipeline explicitly targets the data-quality half of the problem, survivorship bias and backtest overfitting included; because their environments replay recorded data inside a general-purpose Python process, leak-freedom is a property of pipeline discipline rather than of interface shape, and a caller who reaches around the wrapper is not stopped by anything. ABIDES-Gym wraps the ABIDES discrete-event market simulator in Gym environments, bringing multi-agent market microstructure to RL training; its focus is simulation fidelity, and to our knowledge it does not target cross-runtime byte-determinism or tamper-evident trajectories. TradeMaster is a holistic platform spanning data, environments, algorithms and evaluation for RL trading research; it standardizes the workflow rather than hardening the producer, and its evaluation toolkit runs inside the same trust domain as the agent, which is the arrangement \cref{sec:protocol-scoring} declines. mbt\_gym provides model-based Gym environments for limit-order-book problems, including the same Avellaneda--Stoikov family as \cref{sec:tasks}, with closed-form solutions available as baselines; it is the closest relative of the market-making task specifically, and \cref{sec:tasks} notes the relation. Where these descriptions rest on our reading of the systems' papers and repositories rather than on properties their authors claim, we have kept them descriptive; none of these systems, to our knowledge, sets out to make look-ahead structurally unavailable at the interface, pin cross-runtime byte-identity with committed hashes, or make submitted trajectories third-party recomputable, which is the specific combination this paper contributes.

\paragraph{Market simulation and its validation.} ABIDES \citep{byrd2020abides} is the closest simulator in spirit: a high-fidelity multi-agent market with a matching engine and background agent populations. SharpeArena trades some of that fidelity for properties ABIDES does not target, listed in \cref{tab:related}. The microstructure models are the classical ones: Kyle's permanent impact \citep{kyle1985continuous}, Almgren--Chriss temporary impact and execution cost \citep{almgren2001optimal}, and the Avellaneda--Stoikov analytical quoting reference \citep{avellaneda2008high}. The realism diagnostic compares the generator with selected stylized facts from \citet{cont2001empirical}, and \cref{sec:validation} argues from the validation literature \citep{platt2018agent,fagiolo2019validation,lamperti2018information,vyetrenko2020getreal,nagy2025lobbench} that a calibrated gate a public generator can fail is worth more than an uncalibrated one it passes. That literature is also where SharpeArena's gate stops: it is a directional three-check conjunction, not a distributional divergence in the sense of \citet{lamperti2018information} or a full L1-style benchmark suite in the sense of \citet{nagy2025lobbench}, and adopting one of those is the natural next revision of the diagnostic.

\paragraph{Scoring.} The environment deliberately contains no scorer. The deflated Sharpe \citep{bailey2014deflated} and the reliability and process gates that consume this environment's trajectories are specified in the SharpeBench paper \citep{toca2026sharpebench}; this paper is the producer half of that funnel.
