\subsection{Normalized-flow concavity: a falsifiable symmetric probe}\label{sec:f5-concave}

The linear F5 sweep is primarily a consistency check under the model assumptions of \citet{huberman2004price,gatheral2010no}. To make the probe capable of returning a positive, the engine supplies an opt-in permanent-impact exponent on dimensionless crowd flow $x=Q/V$:
\[
I_\kappa(Q;V)=\lambda\,\operatorname{sign}(x)|x|^\kappa,
\qquad x=Q/V.
\]
The temporary own-size term remains $\eta q_i/V$. This normalization is load-bearing: applying a power to raw shares and dividing by $V$ afterwards would change units with $\kappa$ and make results depend on the arbitrary share denomination. A regression test rescales capital, flow and $V$ together and requires the nonlinear impact fraction to remain unchanged. At $\kappa=1$ the engine uses the original expression $(\lambda Q+\eta q_i)/V$ exactly, preserving every linear golden bit; only $\kappa\ne1$ enters the \texttt{powf} path outside the cross-runtime golden guarantee.

We rerun the symmetric push-and-unwind sweep at $\kappa\in\{0.5,0.7\}$, eight seeds per point, with paired zero-impact references and pointwise $t$ intervals. No sampled mean is positive. At the canonical calibration, impact-attributed P\&L is $-8.9\times10^{-4}$ (95\% CI $[-19.6,1.8]\times10^{-4}$) for $\kappa=0.5$, $-13.5\times10^{-4}$ (CI $[-21.2,-5.9]\times10^{-4}$) for $\kappa=0.7$, and $-3.4\times10^{-4}$ (CI $[-4.3,-2.6]\times10^{-4}$) for the linear arm. For $\kappa=0.5$, 7 of 23 stored sweep slots have intervals crossing zero; repeated canonical slots reduce these to five distinct configurations. At $\kappa=0.7$, 0 of 23 cross zero. These are finite-grid, pointwise summaries of this symmetric schedule, not a universal no-manipulation result.
