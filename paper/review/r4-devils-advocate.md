## Strongest Counter-Argument

Grant everything the tests prove and the paper still rests on a contradiction between its two headline properties. Byte-determinism means every scenario is a pure function of one 64-bit seed, computed by an open-source generator deliberately restricted to multiply, add, divide and max. Leak-freedom means the agent cannot call an API that returns a future bar. But in a deterministic environment whose transition function is public, simple, and independent of the agent's actions, the future is not private information: it is a computable function of the observed prefix. An agent that filters over generator states, or simply learns the four-operation transform, acquires the future bar without ever calling a forbidden API. The paper cites the ALE lesson that determinism invites memorization and answers it with held-out seed bands, but seed bands only block lookup by seed identity; they do nothing against inversion of a low-complexity generator from the trailing window, and the paper performs no analysis of that attack. So "leak-free by construction" is a statement about the interface, quietly presented as a statement about information.

The empirical half inherits a second contradiction. The paper builds a realism gate specifically so that agents cannot "win on unrealistic markets", then reports that the canonical leaderboard configuration passes that gate on 1 of 24 runs, keeps the failing configuration as the default, and proceeds to derive every finding, the honest zero, the transfer gaps, the ecology winner, from the tape its own instrument refuses to certify. A reviewer need not dispute a single number to conclude that the paper's conclusions are grounded in an environment the paper itself grades as not believable, measured by trivial policies the paper itself calls unable to test the properties that matter.

## Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. Generator inversion as the dominant "leak": the paper considers seed-as-feature and API peeking, but not that a sufficiently capable learner models the public four-operation transform and predicts the future in-band. Under this explanation, a high leaderboard score on held-out seeds measures generator identification, not trading skill, and the leak-free guarantee is orthogonal to the thing it is meant to protect.
2. F5's universal unprofitability may reflect the specification's inability to represent profitable manipulation (linear impact, one-parameter followers) rather than a well-priced book. A spec engineered to reward pumping, shown to be flagged, would discriminate between "the book prices manipulation" and "the probe cannot see it". The limitations gesture at this ("fail realistically for the wrong reason") but the abstract still says "certifies".
3. F1's tier collapse (DSR 1.00 to 0.02) may be mostly a volatility-denominator effect on the same drift rather than a statement about strategy robustness; the paper reads it as the eligibility conjunction working, but a per-tier Sharpe decomposition (mean vs vol) would distinguish the two.
4. F8's winner swap may be an artifact of the innovation operator breeding only the current leader's variants, which entrenches whichever species leads when the regime turns; a neutral innovation policy could preserve kelly_vol_target through the shock.

## Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- External agent implementers/vendors: the parties the contract governance exists for; their cost of conformance, appetite for JSON-over-stdio loops, and view of the additive-only policy are absent (the paper concedes zero exist).
- Leaderboard hosts/operators: multi-tenant isolation, intake, and abuse handling are named as nonexistent but no operator requirements inform the design.
- Practitioners concerned with sim2real: no perspective on whether skill against this generator family transfers to any market, beyond the one-line limitation.

## Unexamined Premise

The paper assumes that verifiability properties (leak-free API, byte-determinism, replay) are the binding constraint on trading-agent evaluation, and that realism can be deferred ("a future generator revision must add clustering"). The opposite ordering is at least as defensible: an environment can be perfectly verifiable and still measure nothing, and the paper's own F4 result is evidence for that ordering. No argument is offered for why verifiability-first, realism-later is the right sequencing rather than the sequencing that was easiest to build.

## Observations (Non-Defects)

- The paper's willingness to publish its own gate failing (F4) and to label the F3 within-tier gaps as pure noise is genuinely unusual and creditable; the criticism above is about what is built on top of those admissions, not about their honesty.
- The replay-and-tamper test (doctor every order, assert divergence) is a real adversarial test, not a claimed one, and the mul/add/div/max determinism discipline is a clean piece of engineering whether or not it is "more than a nicety".
- Committed evidence JSON matches the reported F4 numbers on direct inspection (both baseline `tiers` and `clustered_tiers` blocks present in paper/evidence/f4-realism.json), which supports the reproducibility posture even where the guarantees are narrower than advertised.
- The DeferredDesk construction (no code path from claim to resolving datum) is an elegant generalization of the leak-free idea and deserves more prominence than it gets.
